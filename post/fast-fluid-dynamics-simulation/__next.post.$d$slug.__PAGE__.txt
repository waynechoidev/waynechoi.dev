<h2>0. Mathematics</h2>
<p>Before diving into the implementation of fluid simulation, it's important to briefly explore the mathematical foundations that underpin fluid dynamics. The primary equation that governs the motion of fluids is the Navier-Stokes equation.</p>
<h3>Navier-Stokes Equation</h3>
<p>The Navier-Stokes equation is a partial differential equation that describes the conservation of momentum for fluid flow. In the case of incompressible fluids, it can be expressed as:</p>
<img src="/img/fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-01.svg" class="latex-long" loading="lazy" /><ul>
<li><strong>𝑢</strong> represents the velocity field of the fluid.</li>
<li><strong>𝑡</strong> represents time.</li>
<li><strong>𝜌</strong> is the density of the fluid.</li>
<li><strong>𝑝</strong> represents pressure.</li>
<li><strong>𝜈</strong> is the kinematic viscosity (a measure of the fluid's resistance to flow).</li>
<li><strong>F</strong> represents external forces (such as gravity).</li>
</ul>
<p>In this context, the term <strong>𝑓</strong> representing external forces is included in the equation but will be omitted in the subsequent discussion for simplicity. This equation serves as the core mathematical tool for describing the motion and behavior of fluids. By solving the Navier-Stokes equation, we can compute the fluid's velocity, pressure, and other properties, which are then used to simulate fluid motion.</p>
<p>To solve this, partial differential equations need to be numerically solved. The methods for this were explained in <a href="https://waynechoi.dev/post/heat-equation/" class="md-link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">a previous post</a> where the heat equation was implemented, which can be reviewed for more details.</p>

<h2>2. Solve Navier-Stokes</h2>
<h3>2.1 Projection</h3>
<p>The projection step involves calculating the pressure field and adjusting the velocity field to ensure the fluid flow is accurately simulated. This process aims to make the divergence of the velocity zero and uses the Poisson equation and Jacobi iteration to iteratively update the pressure field.</p>
<h4>2.1.1 Divergence</h4>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)
fn <span class="hljs-title function_">main</span>(<span class="hljs-params">@builtin(global_invocation_id) id: vec3u</span>) {
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> x = id.<span class="hljs-property">x</span>;
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> y = id.<span class="hljs-property">y</span>;
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> idx = <span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(id.<span class="hljs-property">xy</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>);

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> <span class="hljs-attr">N</span>:<span class="hljs-title class_">Neighbors</span> = <span class="hljs-title function_">getNeighbors</span>(x, y, size);

    divergence_buffer[idx] = <span class="hljs-number">0.25</span> *
    (velocity_buffer[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">right</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)].<span class="hljs-property">x</span> - velocity_buffer[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">left</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)].<span class="hljs-property">x</span> +
    velocity_buffer[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">up</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)].<span class="hljs-property">y</span> - velocity_buffer[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">down</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)].<span class="hljs-property">y</span>);

    pressure_buffer[idx] = <span class="hljs-number">0</span>;
    temp_pressure_buffer[idx] = <span class="hljs-number">0</span>;
}</code></pre><p>Calculating the divergence of the velocity field is a crucial step for pressure calculation. If there is divergence, the velocity field may not converge or may exhibit divergence, which requires pressure to correct.</p>
<p>In this case, since we are calculating the divergence on the vector field of velocity, the formula differs from that used in the heat equation. In the heat equation, the divergence was calculated from the vector field of pressure, which required the use of the Laplacian. However, since we are dealing with a vector field (velocity) that is already in gradient form, we can directly calculate the divergence without needing the Laplacian.</p>
<p>To prepare for the next step, the pressure buffers were initialized here.</p>
<h4>2.1.2 pressure disturbance</h4>

These disturbances can arise from changes in fluid velocity or external forces. To address pressure imbalances, the Poisson equation is used and numerically solved through Jacobi iteration. Jacobi iteration repeatedly updates the pressure field to minimize pressure disturbances, thereby adjusting the velocity field to accurately simulate the fluid's flow.</p>

<h2>3. Viscous Diffusion</h2>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)

<h2>4. Post process</h2>
<p>While not directly related to solving the Navier-Stokes equations, it plays a crucial role in making the simulation results more realistic and stable.</p>
<h3>4.1 Density diffusion</h3>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> <span class="hljs-attr">N</span>:<span class="hljs-title class_">Neighbors</span> = <span class="hljs-title function_">getNeighbors</span>(x, y, size);

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> density = (density_input[idx]
    + constant.<span class="hljs-property">viscosity</span> * constant.<span class="hljs-property">dt</span> * (
    density_input[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">right</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)] + density_input[<span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(N.<span class="hljs-property">left</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>)]

By gradually adjusting density changes, it helps prevent instability in the simulation. For computational efficiency, it was implemented in the same shader program used for viscous diffusion.</p>
<h3>4.2 Vorticity</h3>
<p>Calculate the vorticity of the fluid to achieve a more realistic flow. By adding the rotation (or vortices) of the fluid, the physical accuracy of the simulation is enhanced.</p>
<h4>4.2.1 Compute vorticity</h4>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)

<h2>5. Textures</h2>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> idx = <span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(id.<span class="hljs-property">xy</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>);

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> <span class="hljs-attr">velocity</span>:vec2f = velocity_buffer[idx];
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> <span class="hljs-attr">density</span>:vec4f = density_buffer[idx];

    <span class="hljs-title function_">textureStore</span>(velocity_texture, vec2&lt;i32&gt;(<span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(x), <span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(y)), <span class="hljs-title function_">encodeVelocity</span>(velocity));

}</code></pre><p>Before performing advection, it is necessary to transfer the density buffer and velocity buffer to textures. This is because when advection is performed using a sampler, interpolation is applied, which allows for efficient extraction of density and velocity values from the textures and leads to more accurate fluid flow simulation. If advection is performed directly on storage buffers without interpolation, irregular results such as the staircasing effect shown in the image below may occur.</p>
<img src="/img/fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-02.jpg" class="post-pic" loading="lazy" /><p>One additional point to note is that when storing velocity in a texture, it must be normalized to the 0–1 range to handle negative values. Otherwise, due to the texture's limitation in storing negative values, the velocity would always point only to the right or upwards. For more stable encoding and decoding, the velocity is always clamped to the range of -1 to 1. The following set of functions is used for these tasks.</p>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js"><span class="hljs-comment">// clamp velocity between -1 and 1</span>
fn <span class="hljs-title function_">clampVelocity</span>(<span class="hljs-attr">v</span>: vec2f) -&gt; vec2f {
    <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">clamp</span>(v, <span class="hljs-title function_">vec2f</span>(-<span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>), <span class="hljs-title function_">vec2f</span>(<span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>));
}

<span class="hljs-comment">// encode velocity with -1..1 range to texture with 0..1 range</span>
fn <span class="hljs-title function_">encodeVelocity</span>(<span class="hljs-attr">v</span>: vec2f) -&gt; vec4f {
    <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">vec4f</span>(v * <span class="hljs-number">0.5</span> + <span class="hljs-number">0.5</span>, <span class="hljs-number">0</span>, <span class="hljs-number">0</span>);
}

<span class="hljs-comment">// decode texture with 0..1 range to velocity with -1..1 range</span>
fn <span class="hljs-title function_">decodeVelocity</span>(<span class="hljs-attr">v</span>: vec4f) -&gt; vec2f {
    <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> v.<span class="hljs-property">xy</span> * <span class="hljs-number">2.0</span> - <span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>;
}</code></pre><h2>6. Advection</h2>
<pre><code class="hljs language-js">@compute @<span class="hljs-title function_">workgroup_size</span>(<span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">16</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1</span>)
fn <span class="hljs-title function_">main</span>(<span class="hljs-params">@builtin(global_invocation_id) id: vec3u</span>) {
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> x = <span class="hljs-title function_">f32</span>(id.<span class="hljs-property">x</span>);
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> y = <span class="hljs-title function_">f32</span>(id.<span class="hljs-property">y</span>);
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> idx = <span class="hljs-title function_">getIdx</span>(id.<span class="hljs-property">xy</span>, size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>);
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> pos = vec2&lt;f32&gt;((x + <span class="hljs-number">0.5</span>)/<span class="hljs-title function_">f32</span>(size.<span class="hljs-property">width</span>), (y + <span class="hljs-number">0.5</span>)/<span class="hljs-title function_">f32</span>(size.<span class="hljs-property">height</span>));

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> velocity = velocity_buffer[idx];

    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> dt = constant.<span class="hljs-property">dt</span> * <span class="hljs-number">0.0001</span>;
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> pos_back = pos - velocity * dt;

    <span class="hljs-keyword">var</span> <span class="hljs-attr">back_velocity</span>:vec2f = <span class="hljs-title function_">decodeVelocity</span>(<span class="hljs-title function_">textureSampleLevel</span>(temp_velocity_texture, my_sampler, pos_back, <span class="hljs-number">0</span>));
    back_velocity = <span class="hljs-title function_">clampVelocity</span>(back_velocity);
    <span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> <span class="hljs-attr">back_density</span>:vec4f = <span class="hljs-title function_">textureSampleLevel</span>(temp_density_texture, my_sampler, pos_back, <span class="hljs-number">0</span>);

    <span class="hljs-title function_">textureStore</span>(velocity_texture, vec2&lt;i32&gt;(<span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(x), <span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(y)), <span class="hljs-title function_">encodeVelocity</span>(back_velocity));
    <span class="hljs-title function_">textureStore</span>(density_texture, vec2&lt;i32&gt;(<span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(x), <span class="hljs-title function_">i32</span>(y)), back_density);

    velocity_buffer[idx] = <span class="hljs-title function_">clampVelocity</span>(back_velocity);
    density_buffer[idx] = back_density;
}</code></pre><p>Advection describes the process by which fluids or other physical quantities move according to their velocity. Simply put, it deals with how substances move with the flow of the fluid. Here, the Semi-Lagrangian method was used to numerically solve advection. This method involves tracking the movement of material particles according to the velocity field and calculating their values at new positions.</p>
<p>In this process, to handle the movement of the material, the position was adjusted by adding 0.5 to each axis, starting from the center of the grid. Finally, to render the results, the target texture and the velocity and density buffers for the next frame's calculations are updated. To prevent data race conditions, the density texture and render target texture are kept separate.</p>
<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>By following this sequence in fluid simulation, you can effectively model the physical properties and flow of the fluid. Each step enhances the accuracy of the simulation and helps to accurately represent the dynamic characteristics of the fluid.</p>
