In this post, I will introduce the theoretical background of the Heat Equation, the method of solving it numerically, and how I implemented it in WGSL shader code.</p>

<img src="/img/heat-equation-01.svg" class="latex" loading="lazy" /><p>Here, u(i, j, t) represents the temperature distribution, and α is the thermal conductivity. This equation describes how the distribution of heat changes over time.</p>

<h2>Divergence</h2>
<p>Divergence provides a scalar value that indicates the extent of expansion or contraction in a vector field. In 2D, divergence is defined as follows:</p>
<img src="/img/heat-equation-03.svg" class="latex" loading="lazy" /><p>Here, F is the vector field. Divergence measures how much the vector field spreads out or converges at a given point.</p>
<h2>Laplacian</h2>
<p>The Laplacian is defined as the divergence of the gradient and represents the curvature of a scalar field. In 2D, the Laplacian is defined as follows:</p>
<img src="/img/heat-equation-04.svg" class="latex" loading="lazy" /><p>The Laplacian measures the rate of temperature change at a given point and is a crucial component of the Heat Equation.</p>
<h2>Analytical Methods vs. Numerical Methods</h2>
<p>Analytical methods involve solving mathematical equations exactly. These methods can provide precise solutions for specific equations, but they may not be practical for complex or multidimensional problems. Partial differential equations, such as the Heat Equation, are often difficult to solve analytically, which is why numerical methods are used to obtain approximate solutions.</p>
<p>Numerical methods involve using computers to approximate solutions to equations. This approach discretizes the equations and uses iterative calculations to find approximate solutions. The advantage of numerical methods is that they are practical and efficient for finding solutions to complex problems.</p>

</li>
<li><p>Laplacian Calculation: Update the temperature values at each grid point. <p>The numerical approach to the Heat Equation is an effective method for solving complex problems where analytical solutions are difficult to obtain. These numerical approaches can be applied not only to fluid simulations but also to various physical phenomena such as heat conduction and electric field calculations.</p>
